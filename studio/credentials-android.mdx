Android requires a Firebase project for push notifications and, optionally, a Google Play service account for automated store submission.

**For New Projects**

> _If you already have an existing firebase project and your app already in your project skip to next section - For Existing Projects_

<video controls className="w-full aspect-video rounded-xl" src="https://blockli-app-videos.b-cdn.net/firebase_admin_sdk_setup.mp4" />

1. Go to [console.firebase.google.com](http://console.firebase.google.com) and sign in with your Google account.
2. Click **Create a project**. It may say "Add project" on older layouts.
3. Enter a **project name**, such as your app or company name. Firebase generates a unique project ID underneath, and you can edit it before you continue.
4. Click **Continue** on the Gemini/AI assistance screen if it appears, then choose whether to enable **Google Analytics**. Studio doesn't need it, so you can turn it off. Click **Create project**.
5. Wait for the setup to finish, then click **Continue**. You land on the project home.

You can also use an existing Google Cloud project, such as the one you used for Play, by choosing it under the project name. That's optional. A separate Firebase project is fine.

**Get the Service Account JSON**

1. Click the **gear icon** next to "Project Overview" in the top left, then **Project settings**.
2. Open the **Service accounts** tab.
3. Make sure **Firebase Admin SDK** is selected, then click **Generate new private key**.
4. Confirm with **Generate key**. A JSON file downloads straight away, and Google doesn't keep a copy. Keep it somewhere safe.

### For Existing Projects

<video controls className="w-full aspect-video rounded-xl" src="https://blockli-app-videos.b-cdn.net/adding-service-json-for-existing-app.mp4" />

1. Open [Firebase Console](https://console.firebase.google.com) and select (or create) your project.
2. Go to **Project Settings → Services Accounts**.
3. Make sure **Firebase Admin SDK** is selected, then click **Generate new private key**.
4. Confirm with **Generate key**. A JSON file downloads straight away, and Google doesn't keep a copy. Keep it somewhere safe.

### Uploading to Studio

Upload the `google-services.json` file in the **google-services.json** field.

## Service Account Admin SDK JSON

Required for Blockli to relay **Firebase Cloud Messaging v1** push notifications.

### Getting the file

1. In [Firebase Console](https://console.firebase.google.com), go to **Project Settings → Service accounts**.
2. Click **Generate new private key**.
3. Confirm and download the JSON file.

### **Upload it in Studio**

1. Go to Store Setup → Credentials, on the Android card.
2. In **Service account (admin SDK) JSON**, click **Upload service account JSON** and choose the file.
3. Click **Save & verify**. You should see "✓ Connected to Firebase". A brand-new project will report 0 Android apps.
4. Go to the **Blockli project** tab and use **Choose** on the Android field. Studio loads the list of existing package names in your project. Select your release and test apps and save.

## Google Play API

Required for automated submission to the Play Store (optional if you prefer to upload manually).

<video controls className="w-full aspect-video rounded-xl" src="https://blockli-app-videos.b-cdn.net/play_store_submission_srvice-account.mp4" />

### Getting the credentials

1. **Open Cloud Console:** go to [console.cloud.google.com](https://console.cloud.google.com/) and sign in with the Google account that owns your Play Console, or one that has access to the project.
2. **Check the project:** the project name shows in the top bar. Use the one where you enabled the Play Android Developer API. If it's a different one, click the name and switch. The service account and the API must be in the same project.
3. **Open Service Accounts:** in the left menu choose **IAM & Admin → Service Accounts**. If you don't see the menu, click the ☰ icon at the top left. You can also search "Service Accounts" in the top search bar.
4. **Start creating:** click **\+ Create service account** at the top.
5. **Step 1, service account details:**

   - **Name:** something clear like `blockli-play-publisher`. The ID fills in automatically.
   - **Description:** optional, e.g. "Uploads builds to Google Play from Blockli Studio".
   - Click **Create and continue**.
6. **Step 2, grant this service account access to the project:** skip it. Play permissions are set in Play Console, not here. Click **Continue**.
7. **Step 3, grant users access to this service account:** skip it too, and click **Done**.
8. **Find the email:** you're back on the list and the new account is there. Copy its **email**, which looks like `blockli-play-publisher@your-project.iam.gserviceaccount.com`. You'll paste it into Play Console when you invite it as a user.

The account has no key yet, so the next step is to click its name, open the **Keys** tab and add a JSON key.

**Download the JSON key**

1. In **IAM & Admin → Service Accounts**, click the name (or email) of the account you just created.
2. Open the **Keys** tab.
3. Click **Add key → Create new key**.
4. Choose **JSON** and click **Create**. The file downloads to your computer straight away. Google doesn't keep a copy of it, so store it somewhere safe.

**Upload it to Studio**

1. Go to Store Setup → Credentials, on the Android card.
2. In the **Google Play service account (.json)** field, click **Upload Play .json** and choose the file.
3. Click **Save**.

**Give it access in Play Console** 

1. Open Play Console → **Users and permissions** → **Invite new users**.
2. Paste the service account's email, the one ending in `.iam.gserviceaccount.com`.
3. Under app permissions, choose your app, or account-level permissions, and grant what you need. Uploading builds needs release rights, or Admin.
4. Click **Invite user**. A service account has no inbox, so there's no email to accept.

The invite can take a while to take effect, so if Play uploads fail with a permissions error right after, wait and try again.

<Warning>
  The Play service account field in Studio only stores the file, so there's no connection check like the Apple and Firebase ones. You'll find out it works on the first Play upload.
</Warning>
